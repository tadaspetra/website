From 2018 to 2022, I became one of the top Flutter influencers, but this was never my goal. It never felt right to be associated with one specific technological niche.

The real goal when I started was to build an app for my book club that my friends could use on both iOS and Android devices. Flutter allows you to write your app in one language (Dart) and compile to both operating systems.

So I chose to build this app using Flutter.

Before I could build, I needed to learn Flutter. My favorite way to learn was binging YouTube tutorials. But I ran into a problem. Since Flutter was still in beta, there was not much content on YouTube. 

I thought there should be. 

So, I took it upon myself to start making some. The videos were terrible at the beginning, but then they got better, and the audience started growing. I kept improving my skills and eventually amassed an audience of 50,000 across multiple platforms, got recognized as a Google Developer Expert for Flutter & Dart, and became a Flutter Developer Advocate at Agora.

This was nice, but it was never my goal.

Throughout this journey, I fell in love with teaching. Even though I had no previous experience with formal teaching, I realized I’ve been doing it informally for most of my life with family members and friends. And I was relatively good at it. I love the challenge of getting to the core of a problem, then expressing that core clearly and simply.

YouTube was an incredible feedback loop for building this skill. If something wasn’t clear, I could always trust the comments to correct me. Usually in a kind way, sometimes in a not-so-kind way.

## The Reinforcement Loop

Creating these videos was incredibly fulfilling because the result was a deep knowledge of the topics I made videos about. Since I started with Flutter, a reinforcement loop was created to continue making content about Flutter.

```mermaid
flowchart TD
 A["Choose Flutter Niche"] --> B["Make Flutter Video"]
 B --> C["Learn topic"]
 C --> D["Audience resonates"]
 D --> E["Video performs well"]
 E -. reinforces .-> B
```

Initially, this was great, and I was too busy to think about anything other than how to make my videos less shitty. But eventually, I felt stuck. 

The reason I started this journey was that I wanted to build something, but I had learned enough for what I needed to build Flutter apps. 

At this point, the book club app no longer excited me. I was most excited about building websites like my personal website or an education platform. Behind the scenes, I was building and learning about web development and backend development. I tried to create content related to this, but it didn't perform well since my audience was a Flutter audience. At this moment in my career, I was a new DevRel focused on Flutter, so having my content do well was directly tied to success at my job. I had a really supportive leader (shoutout Hermes the GOAT), so I could have experimented more, but internally, I want to add value to the company I worked for.

I stayed in this limbo mode of only building projects for work with Flutter and still kinda creating mostly exclusively educational Flutter content, but learning and building websites in my spare time.

Since then, I ended up expanding my role to include web technologies, built a startup on the web, and joined an AI company focused on all SDKs.

But something inside still identifies as within the Flutter niche. It's time for me to move on.

This isn't a reflection on Flutter as a technology. It's a reflection of my goal to build things. Some of the things might be physical things, some of them might be software, some of them might be apps, and some of those apps might even be built with Flutter.

## Reflections on this journey

My meta goal is to live life without any regrets. 

Maybe I could have done some things differently, but maybe if I did, I wouldn't be where I am today. Maybe I would be in a *better* place? Maybe in a *worse* place? 

There are an infinite number of paths life can take, and I am content with the path I'm on. For all I know, this path I have taken might be the most optimal path I could have taken.

1. Find a project you are curious about.
2. Go really deep in one specific niche of that project.
3. Learn a lot of skills along the way.
4. Apply those skills to a wider project.
5. Repeat.



```mermaid
flowchart TD
 A["Find a project"] --> B["Go deep"]
 B --> C["Learn skills"]
 C -. Repeat .-> A
```

If I were to advise my past self, the only thing I would change is to enjoy the journey a little more.

## My opinions about Flutter

Again, my decision to create other things is not a reflection of Flutter as a technology.

Flutter is cool. It's a great way to build apps for any platform. I plan to continue to choose it whenever it makes sense.

Lately, I've been building a lot of websites. Websites are different from web apps, and Flutter is not a good choice for websites. I've built sites in React, Svelte, NextJS, SvelteKit, SolidJS, Astro, etc. My favorite web framework is Astro, and I mostly use React components for reactivity.

I really enjoy building Astro websites and have grown to tolerate writing React. I believe it's mostly due to Tailwind. Having your styling short, easy to read, and on one line is nice. Still, there are many parts that I don't enjoy with React. But this enjoyment of Tailwind led me to recently try React Native with Nativewind. And I actually kind of like it.

Native components also feel like a bigger deal than I initially thought. I personally don't really like Apple's Liquid Glass as a consumer (mostly due to the long animations and flashbangs), but building with Liquid Glass components makes your apps feel premium.

Also, Expo not only makes it easier to deploy and work with React Native apps, but it might actually fulfill the dream of having one codebase for websites and apps. There is still a lot more work needed, but server-side rendering, middleware, and other modern web necessities are in public alpha.

Flutter has benefits as well. The developer tooling is incredible and I have complete confidence in it to find the root issue fast. Also having desktop (including Linux) and even embedded system support feels like true cross platform.

I'm not choosing sides. You can build great products with anything. The choice for what you use depends on the project you are working on and the people you are working with. If I were to choose a side, it would be the side of whoever builds the cooler thing.

## My Future

The main goal for me is not to restrain myself from exploring deeply, building what excites me, and sharing things that I am curious about. That means there will be some deep dives on topics that I have wanted to cover for years.

I also want to continue improving my skills while not worrying about the numbers as much. Instead, focus on the quality of the output and trust that if the quality continues to improve, the numbers will follow.
